# EcoLoop

**Trade what you have. Build what you need.**

A community marketplace where students exchange unused belongings and find the materials for their projects, while the people supplying those materials receive value they can use elsewhere in the community.

**Complete revised concept and hackathon plan**  
**Prepared for:** Pratyush Dutta  
**Date:** 2 October 2026  
**Status:** Proposed product design. Features and examples below describe what to build; they do not claim an existing implementation or completed pilot.

---

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

EcoLoop combines ordinary item trading with a project builder. A student can browse the marketplace and exchange books, stationery, supplies, or other useful belongings. Alternatively, they can enter a project name, a description, and a list of required items. The application searches the community's available listings, matches suitable materials from different owners, and assembles a proposed project kit. The student contributes equivalent accepted value to the marketplace, and each material owner receives their agreed share as trade credits. Those credits let owners obtain other available items they actually want, including materials for their own projects.

**One platform. Two main sections: Marketplace and Projects.**

## 2. The problem EcoLoop addresses

A student wants to make a desk organiser, a school exhibition model, a costume, or a prototype. They know what they want to build but have to find the materials separately. Meanwhile, other students have unused supplies or suitable leftover materials that could meet those requirements.

Ordinary barter becomes difficult when several people are involved. The student might offer a geometry box, while the cardboard owner wants a book and the fabric owner wants art supplies. Finding materials alone does not solve that exchange problem.

EcoLoop connects three needs:

| Need | EcoLoop's response |
|---|---|
| “I have something useful that I no longer need.” | List it for direct barter or an exchange using trade credits. |
| “I need several things for a particular project.” | Submit one requirement list and receive a kit assembled from available listings. |
| “Why should I supply materials if I do not want the maker's offered item?” | Receive credits that can be spent on other participating listings or shared stock. |

The goal is to make existing resources easier to use again and make contributing worthwhile for every participant.

## 3. The two main sections

### A. Marketplace

This keeps the normal EcoTrade experience: users list what they have, browse what others offer, and arrange exchanges.

Listings can include books, stationery, art supplies, hobby materials, clean containers, spare components, reusable packaging, and finished handmade items. Suitable damaged items can be listed for their usable parts, with the condition clearly described.

Owners choose their exchange preference:

- **Direct barter:** Accept a particular item or an agreed bundle in return.
- **Trade credits:** Accept an agreed number of credits that can be used elsewhere in EcoLoop.
- **Gift:** Give the item away voluntarily, without receiving credits.

The listing shows photos, condition, quantity, relevant dimensions, approximate location or school community, and the owner's exchange preference. A material listing also specifies its material type and usable portion.

An owner can update an available listing, accept or decline a request, and choose whether it can be considered for project kits.

### B. Projects

This section starts with **what the student wants to make**.

The student enters:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Project name | Desktop organiser |
| Description | An organiser for pens, notes, and small stationery, made using reused materials. |
| Required items | Three cardboard sheets, three cardboard tubes, fabric scraps, and string. |
| Specifications | Minimum sheet dimensions, tube size, and required fabric area. |
| Already available | Glue and scissors. |
| Needed by | A chosen collection date. |
| Acceptable alternatives | Different fabric colours are fine; a smaller base needs approval. |
| Community | My school. |

Users can write their own requirements or start from a project template. The application searches for the items they specify; the user remains in control of the project's requirements.

## 4. How a project becomes a material kit

1. **Describe the project.** The student adds its name, description, and material checklist.
2. **Check the requirements.** The app asks for missing quantities, units, dimensions, or compatibility details.
3. **Search the marketplace.** It looks for available listings that satisfy each requirement and whose owners allow project requests.
4. **Build a proposed kit.** It shows the matching items, owners, quantities, total credit cost, and collection arrangements.
5. **Review gaps and alternatives.** The student approves substitutions and sees any requirements that remain unfilled.
6. **Arrange the contribution.** The student supplies accepted items of sufficient value or uses credits already earned from earlier contributions or exchanges.
7. **Request the kit.** Owners approve their respective quantities and agreed credit amounts. The app reserves the items and holds the student's credits.
8. **Confirm collection.** For the school pilot, a collection point checks the materials and hands over the complete kit. Settlement transfers credits to each owner.
9. **Build and record the result.** The student can upload the finished project and return useful leftovers to the marketplace.

**“Fetch the materials” means find, request, reserve, and coordinate collection.** The app cannot take someone's item without their acceptance, manufacture missing stock, or guarantee delivery merely because a listing exists.

## 5. The central solution: trade credits

### How the original material owners benefit

Each supplier receives spendable trade credits equal to the value agreed for their contribution. These credits work across participating EcoLoop listings and the community's shared stock.

For example, a student supplying cardboard might spend their credits on a geometry box. Another supplying fabric could choose a novel. Neither needs to want the same item the project maker contributed.

**The maker can receive a kit from several owners. Each owner receives their own share of the exchange value and chooses what to obtain in return.**

Credits are units for exchanging goods inside the pilot. They have no promised rupee conversion, cash withdrawal, investment return, or automatic expiry during the pilot. Ordinary barter remains available to people who prefer a specific item in return.

### Where a student's first credits come from

For the initial school pilot, use a small **community exchange shelf** managed by the organising club or a designated volunteer.

The student brings an item or bundle they no longer need. The organiser checks its condition, usefulness, and proposed credit value. If the student and organiser agree, the items enter the shared stock, and the student receives that accepted value in credits.

The student can then spend those credits on the project kit. The material owners receive the same credits through the exchange and can redeem them for shared stock or use them with other members who accept credits.

This allows a maker to contribute one or more unrelated useful items in exchange for materials from several people.

**Uploading a listing does not earn credits. The contribution must be physically accepted into shared stock, or another member must complete an exchange that transfers existing credits to the contributor.**

### What makes the credits credible

The pilot distinguishes three events:

| Event | What happens to credits |
|---|---|
| An organiser accepts goods into shared stock | The contributor receives new credits equal to the agreed stock value. |
| One member obtains another member's item | Existing credits transfer from the recipient to the owner. No new credits are created. |
| A member redeems goods from shared stock | The member's spent credits are retired as the goods leave the stock. |

The organising team tracks issued credits against available shared-stock value. Goods held only temporarily for a project pickup are separate from shared stock and do not generate a second credit award.

Shared-stock acceptance transfers the item into the exchange pool. Its previous owner cannot withdraw it while keeping the credits. Before those credits are committed or spent, a reversal is possible only if the item is still available and the matching credits are removed.

This stock management is part of the operating model. If the pilot has no organiser or storage point, immediate credits for unsold contributions should be disabled. Students would first need a completed exchange that transfers existing credits, or an agreed direct barter.

## 6. A complete example: everyone receives something useful

All values below are illustrative demo values, not market prices.

### Step 1: A student requests a desk organiser kit

| Required material | Supplier | Agreed credit value |
|---|---|---:|
| Three suitable cardboard sheets | Asha | 20 |
| Three cardboard tubes | Kabir | 15 |
| A fabric-and-string bundle | Mira | 25 |
| **Total** | **Three owners** | **60** |

Glue and scissors are already available to the maker, so they are listed separately and excluded from the kit's cost.

### Step 2: The maker provides equivalent value

The maker contributes these accepted items to the exchange shelf:

| Contributed item | Accepted credit value |
|---|---:|
| A complete, usable geometry box | 20 |
| An unused sketch pad | 15 |
| A readable novel | 25 |
| **Total contribution** | **60** |

The maker now has 60 credits. The shared stock contains 60 credits' worth of accepted goods.

### Step 3: The project exchange completes

The suppliers approve the request, bring their materials to the collection point, and the maker checks and collects the kit. The maker's 60 credits are distributed as follows:

- Asha receives 20 credits.
- Kabir receives 15 credits.
- Mira receives 25 credits.

The maker's balance becomes zero. No additional credits are created when the suppliers receive their shares.

### Step 4: The suppliers choose their benefit

In this example, Asha chooses the geometry box, Kabir chooses the sketch pad, and Mira chooses the novel. As those items leave shared stock, the corresponding credits are retired.

They could instead keep their credits and select other available items later. They do not have to choose from this particular maker's contribution.

The completed exchange has a clear result: **one student obtains a project kit, three suppliers obtain useful belongings, and existing goods and materials find new users.**

## 7. What counts as an equivalent contribution?

Equivalent means **agreed exchange value**, rather than an equal number of objects, equal weight, or original purchase price.

For a 60-credit project kit, a student could contribute one accepted 60-credit item, three items totalling 60 credits, or a larger accepted bundle and keep the surplus credits. Credits earned previously can cover some or all of the requirement because value has already been contributed or earned through an earlier exchange.

### Practical valuation for the pilot

- Publish a small set of example credit bands for supported item categories.
- Consider condition, completeness, quantity, specifications, usefulness, and demonstrated local demand.
- Let marketplace owners propose a value; a request proceeds only when both sides agree.
- Have an organiser approve values for goods entering shared stock, because that acceptance creates new credits.
- Fix the agreed value once an exchange is accepted. New prices apply only to future requests.

Do not let a user assign themselves unlimited credits for an unwanted item. A photographed object, a high original price, or a claim that something is rare does not establish an accepted exchange value.

### Prevent the exchange shelf from filling with unwanted items

Start with a narrow set of useful goods, visible wishlists, limited storage, and caps on accepted quantities. Prefer contributions connected to an active request or recent demonstrated demand. Show the student whether their item is eligible before they carry it to school.

If shared stock already has too many similar items or there is little evidence of demand, the item can remain a normal listing without generating immediate credits.

Users should see available stock before agreeing to receive credits. Credits cannot guarantee that a particular desired item will be available. If stock is damaged, lost, or requires a lower valuation, the organiser records the shortfall, pauses further credit issuance, and resolves the stock coverage before restarting it.

**Useful inventory and trustworthy exchanges are the owner's incentive. Badges and project acknowledgements are optional extras.**

## 8. How the matching should work

The matcher turns a requirement list into a proposed kit using actual inventory.

| Check | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| Item or material type | The listing matches the requirement or an explicitly permitted alternative. |
| Quantity and unit | There is enough available material, with compatible units. |
| Dimensions and condition | The usable portion meets the stated specification. |
| Availability | The relevant quantity has not already been reserved or transferred. |
| Owner's preferences | The owner accepts the proposed exchange mode and project requests. |
| Location and deadline | The proposed pickup is practical within the student's community and timeframe. |

After applying these requirements, rank valid options by compatibility, local collection convenience, credit cost, and the number of pickups needed. A cheaper incompatible item must not outrank a suitable one.

One requirement can be fulfilled by several owners. For example, five sheets may come from two listings offering three and two sheets. Quantity updates must prevent another request from reserving those same sheets.

The result should distinguish:

- **Matched:** A suitable available listing was found; owner acceptance is still pending.
- **Alternative:** A substitution needs the student's approval.
- **Missing:** No suitable listing currently satisfies the requirement.
- **Reserved:** The owner has accepted and the relevant quantity is held.

An incomplete kit stays a draft. The app can publish a wanted-item request with the student's permission or notify them when suitable stock appears. It should never silently replace a requirement or present a partially matched kit as complete.

### The role of AI

AI can help turn a description into an editable requirements draft, recognise similar wording, or suggest alternatives for review. The student confirms the final list and specifications.

The core matching, stock checks, owner approvals, and credit accounting should work with ordinary rules and database records. This makes the main experience demonstrable without relying on AI to invent project requirements or available stock.

## 9. Collection, reservations, and cancellations

For the first version, use one school and scheduled collection sessions. A desk, labelled containers, and a shelf can support the physical exchange process if an organiser agrees to manage them.

Keep two types of goods separate:

| Goods at the collection point | Ownership and treatment |
|---|---|
| Accepted contributions in shared stock | Available for redemption; their contributor has already received credits. |
| Materials temporarily held for a project request | Belong to their suppliers until the kit exchange completes; supplier credits remain pending. |

### Use complete-kit settlement for the MVP

The application reserves all kit quantities and holds the required credits after all suppliers accept. Suppliers deliver their materials to the collection point, where condition and quantity are checked. Once the entire kit is ready and the maker accepts the handover, one settlement transfers the held credits to the suppliers and marks the materials as transferred.

Set a visible reservation deadline. If a supplier cancels or a material fails inspection before settlement, offer a replacement or cancel the kit. Cancellation releases the credit hold and reservations; any materials already delivered are returned to their owners. Holding credits does not pay a supplier before a successful handover.

If the maker's earlier contribution has already entered shared stock, cancelling a project restores their available credits. Returning their original contributed object is a separate reversal and is possible only if that object is still available and the credits can be removed.

Start with this complete-kit model. Separate delivery and settlement for individual kit lines can be a later feature.

## 10. Closing the loop after a project

When a project is finished, the maker can:

- Keep and use the result.
- List the finished creation for another agreed exchange.
- List unused materials with updated quantities.
- Share the requirements, completed photos, and practical improvements so another student can reuse the project plan.

A reusable project plan can become a template. The next student opens it and checks which materials are currently available in their community.

With permission, a project's page can acknowledge its material contributors and show what their supplies helped create. This recognition complements their trade credits; it does not replace compensation.

Completing or photographing a project does not automatically generate spendable credits. If the finished item is later exchanged, the recipient pays its newly agreed value through a normal exchange.

## 12. What to build for the hackathon

The MVP should demonstrate one normal exchange and one complete project exchange involving several owners.

| Feature | First working version | Later expansion |
|---|---|---|
| Community | One school or club | Multiple institutions and nearby communities |
| Marketplace | Listings, search, direct barter, and credit exchanges | Saved searches and richer discovery |
| Projects | Custom name, description, and requirement list | A larger reusable project library |
| Matching | Category, quantity, dimensions, and availability checks | Better suggestions and more complex compatibility rules |
| Kit | Matches from several owners with one total | More pickup and delivery options |
| Contributions | Organiser-approved stock intake | More collection locations |
| Credits | Issue, hold, transfer, release, and retire credits | Additional reporting and operating controls |
| Fulfilment | Complete-kit pickup at one point | Separate fulfilment for kit lines |
| Completion | Photos, material use, leftovers, and optional relisting | Reviewed community instructions |
| AI | Optional requirement drafting | Better assistance after the core flow works |

Start with a few easily described project examples: a desk organiser, an exhibition display, and a decorative fabric banner. Enter real dimensions and physically check at least the project used in the demonstration.

For the first pilot, choose clean materials and ordinary projects the team can inspect. Project matching should not imply that an unreviewed design is safe or suitable for every use.

## 13. Technical outline

Build on the existing EcoTrade code where practical, after inspecting its current implementation. This concept does not assume which frameworks or features are already present.

The main additions are a project requirements form, a listing matcher, a kit reservation flow, a credit ledger, and organiser stock management.

### Suggested records

| Record | Main information |
|---|---|
| User | Identity, community, role, and exchange history |
| Listing | Owner or shared-stock holder, category, condition, specifications, quantity, exchange preference, and value |
| Project | Creator, name, description, deadline, and progress |
| Requirement | Item type, quantity, unit, specifications, and approved substitutions |
| Kit line | Requirement, source listing, supplier, allocated quantity, agreed value, and status |
| Contribution | Offered goods, accepted valuation, intake confirmation, and resulting stock entries |
| Exchange | Participants, accepted terms, reservations, handover, and cancellation state |
| Credit ledger entry | Account, amount, event type, transaction reference, and reversal reference where applicable |
| Credit hold | Project request, held amount, expiry, and settlement state |
| Evidence | Confirmation, photo, measurement, or organiser check supporting a specific outcome |

### Important behaviour

- Only the owner can edit their listing or accept its exchange terms; only an authorised organiser can approve shared-stock intake.
- Calculate available quantities and balances on the server. Users cannot submit their own final credit balance or mark somebody else's handover as complete.
- Reserve quantities and credits together. Recheck availability when committing a kit.
- Settle the kit as one database transaction so every supplier receives the agreed share exactly once.
- Give each settlement a unique reference; repeated clicks or retries must not create duplicate credits.
- Preserve ledger history and record reversals instead of silently editing completed transfers.
- Separate shared stock from temporarily held project materials to prevent double credit issuance.
- Do not expose private messages or precise collection details to unrelated users.

For a member-to-member exchange, the maker's total debit must equal the suppliers' total credit. New credit issuance and retirement are separate events tied to actual shared-stock intake and redemption.

## 14. What makes this worth presenting

The strongest demonstration is the connection between a real project requirement and an exchange that benefits every supplier.

| Element | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| A project becomes a shopping list for reused resources | Students can search around an intended outcome. |
| One kit can draw from several owners | Useful small contributions can be combined. |
| Credits carry value between unrelated exchanges | The maker and every supplier do not need matching wants. |
| Physical acceptance supports first-time contributions | Users cannot obtain materials merely by uploading an unwanted item. |
| Missing stock remains visible | The proposal stays grounded in what the community actually has. |
| Finished projects and leftovers return to the same marketplace | Making and trading remain connected after collection. |

The concept fits a circular-economy project through continued use, exchanging surplus goods, and using suitable leftover materials. Its effectiveness should be demonstrated through completed exchanges and useful projects, without claiming that marketplaces, barter, or reuse are new inventions.

## 15. Measuring success

Track what happened and what participants received.

| Metric | What to record |
|---|---|
| Completed ordinary exchanges | Exchanges with confirmed handover |
| Project fulfilment | Completed kits and requirements left unfilled |
| Time to assemble a kit | Time from confirmed requirements to collection readiness |
| Supplier benefit | Credits received and subsequently spent on useful items |
| Stock usefulness | How long accepted contributions remain unredeemed |
| Project completion | Finished projects with stated evidence level |
| Materials reused | Measured or clearly labelled estimated quantities actually used |
| Continued use | A later participant check on whether the item or project is still useful |

Do not count listings as completed reuse. Do not count the same source material again each time it moves or the finished creation is exchanged. Financial savings can be recorded as participant estimates when there is a clear comparison; automatic carbon-saving figures are outside this MVP.

A particularly useful pilot question is: **“Did the material owners obtain something they wanted?”** If owners accumulate credits but cannot find useful items, the exchange pool needs better supply and demand matching before expansion.

## 16. A practical four-week build plan

| Week | Main work | Demonstrable outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inspect the existing app; complete listings and exchanges; recruit a small pilot group; agree on stock intake and credit bands. | Users can list and request real items. |
| 2 | Add project requirements, inventory matching, and kit review. | One project finds compatible materials from several owners and explains gaps. |
| 3 | Add contribution intake, credit accounting, reservations, and complete-kit settlement. | A verified contribution funds a kit and each supplier receives the correct amount. |
| 4 | Run a small supervised exchange session, build the example project, fix blockers, and rehearse. | The demo shows the entire exchange and real supplier redemption. |

Keep costs low by starting with the existing application, borrowed collection space, labelled containers, and locally available project materials. Physical coordination and organiser time are operating requirements, even when the software is inexpensive to run.

### Critical acceptance checks

1. The same quantity cannot be reserved for two kits.
2. A listed but unaccepted contribution produces no credits.
3. A 60-credit kit pays exactly 60 credits in total to its suppliers, once.
4. A failed or cancelled kit releases its holds and handles physically delivered materials correctly.
5. Shared-stock redemption retires the corresponding credits and removes that stock.
6. Missing specifications and incompatible materials remain visible instead of receiving false matches.
7. User actions cannot change another user's listings, approvals, or balance.

## 18. Short pitch

> EcoLoop helps students trade what they have and build what they need. They can exchange items normally, or describe a project and let the application find its required materials across the community marketplace. To collect the kit, they contribute equivalent accepted value. Every material owner receives their share as trade credits and can choose other useful items in return. A small community exchange shelf supports the first contribution, while the application handles matching, approvals, quantities, and fair settlement. The result is a practical way to connect unused belongings with real project needs and give every contributor a reason to participate.

**EcoLoop — Trade what you have. Build what you need.**
