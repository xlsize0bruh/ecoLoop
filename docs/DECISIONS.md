# Why owners should participate

The owner should not have to want the maker's offered item. Direct barter across three suppliers forces several simultaneous coincidences of wants. A badge or environmental claim does not replace the useful item being given up.

EcoLoop uses an agreed credit value for each owner's actual material contribution. Owners control whether their listing participates, its quantity/value and approval of each request. Payment follows successful handover of the complete kit, so the maker is not charged for an unusable partial kit. The interface shows the payout before consent and the earned balance afterwards.

## Credits need useful goods behind them

The shared shelf solves the first-exchange problem: a member offers a useful item, an organiser physically accepts it, and matching credits are issued against stock. Listing alone never earns credits. These credits transfer to suppliers, who can choose different goods. On shelf redemption the goods and their credit value leave together.

This is an operating commitment, not free money. Reject damaged, incomplete or unwanted contributions. Limit quantities of overstocked categories. Ask owners what they want before accepting more goods. Browseable shelf stock helps suppliers decide whether credits are useful to them. Gifts stay voluntary and direct barter stays available.

## Reservation choices

The draft concept reserves only after all owners accept. This implementation provisionally holds quantities and maker credits when the request is submitted, for a short visible window of at most 48 hours. Each owner then approves their own immutable line. This prevents simultaneous approval flows from promising identical stock. Any participant can cancel before collection. The maker cannot spend held credits; no supplier is paid before complete collection.

Cancelled or expired materials already received at the desk stay unavailable until the organiser confirms they were returned. The UI explicitly calls out these returns. The maker gets available credits back, not an automatic promise to retrieve an original contribution that may already have been redeemed.

## Honest matching

Exact material type and unit, sufficient quantity, minimum dimensions, condition, same community, ownership and stock availability are required. Cheaper compatible options are considered first. Each requirement can draw from several suppliers, but no quantity is counted twice. Every quote is rechecked on request, including supplier identities and prices. Gaps remain drafts. Suggestions and fuzzy substitutions can be added later with explicit user approval.

## Design continuity

Marketplace screens keep EcoTrade's dark neutral interface and plain PHP/JavaScript implementation. Project Studio uses the same typography, borders and compact controls. A restrained yellow-green accent indicates returned value and matched material states. Consistent CSS illustrations show the actual cardboard/tube/fabric example without introducing unrelated stock imagery. Motion respects reduced-motion preferences.

The product prioritises a complete, demonstrable exchange: describe → match → fund through accepted goods → owner consent → checked collection → supplier benefit. It avoids simulated payouts, fabricated impact metrics and a separate incompatible frontend stack.
